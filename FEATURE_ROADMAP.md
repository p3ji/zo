# Piano Pals: ideas for the next releases

Goal: give the player a fresh reason to return while making piano practice feel rewarding. Add a few features at a time and ask which ones she actually enjoys before building more.

## Next: make each practice session feel different

1. **Practice quests.** Pick one small musical goal before playing, such as a tricky bar, gentle dynamics, or a smooth left hand. Mark it done afterward for a badge. Keep the existing daily piano ticket, so longer sessions do not become a ticket race.
2. **Song garden.** Give each song a flower that grows from “learning notes” to “playing smoothly” to “ready to share.” A completed song earns a permanent decoration for the bag.
3. **Gentle return reward.** Celebrate three practice days in a week without requiring consecutive days. Keep the current streak visible, but never punish a missed day.

## Then: expand the creative rewards

4. **Sticker collections.** Release small themed sets (ocean, music, space, seasons) with different shapes and sizes. Let her choose a bag colour and save a few bag layouts.
5. **More colouring books.** Add one original 40 × 40 or larger page every few weeks. Mix easy and intricate pages; offer a free page regularly. Include an optional “show picture” toggle and a finished artwork gallery.
6. **Pet reactions.** The selected animal can cheer after practice, wear earned accessories, and appear in completed colouring scenes. Keep animations brief and optional.

## Later: support musical growth

7. **Mini recital.** Record a short performance only on the device, then let her replay it beside an older recording to hear progress. Recordings stay private unless a grown-up explicitly exports one.
8. **Parent settings.** Let a grown-up adjust goals, ticket values, and real prize availability behind a simple parent gate. Add an export/import backup before any account or sync feature.

## How to choose what ships

After each release, watch two or three practice sessions and ask: “What did you like?”, “What felt boring?”, and “What should your buddy do next?” Prefer the feature she asks for again over a larger feature list. Keep the main practice button one tap away and let creative play be a reward rather than a barrier to practising.
